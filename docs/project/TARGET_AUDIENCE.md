# Target Audience & Customer Insight

## 1. Primary Target
Thai police personnel at operational working levels whose main responsibilities are administrative, coordination, documentation, reporting, meeting support, follow-up, personnel/support tasks, or related office-based functions, and who use LINE regularly for work communication.

## 2. Design-Age Persona
Primary creative lens: approximately 23–40 years old. This is a design/communication persona, not a claim about the full workforce.

## 3. Secondary Target
- More senior or older administrative personnel who use the same utility phrases
- Colleagues who gift occupation-relevant stickers
- Close friends/family who enjoy the character identity

## 4. Core Behavioral Need
The user frequently needs to:
- acknowledge instructions
- request information
- coordinate people or units
- invite or notify people
- join/prepare meetings
- track progress
- report status
- send completed work
- manage urgency
- maintain politeness while communicating quickly
- release work stress with safe peer humor

## 5. Jobs-to-be-Done
### Functional
- Replace repetitive typing
- Communicate status quickly
- Make requests clearer and friendlier
- Confirm receipt/completion
- Signal urgency

### Emotional
- Reduce stiffness in work chat
- Express workload/fatigue without overexplaining
- Feel represented by an occupation-specific character

### Social
- Maintain respect with superiors
- Be friendly with peers
- Create shared humor inside a work group

## 6. Relationship Modes
### Superior-facing
Tone: respectful, concise, reliable.
Examples: รับทราบครับ / เรียบร้อยครับ / กำลังดำเนินการครับ

### Peer-facing
Tone: friendly, efficient, lightly humorous.
Examples: งานเข้าครับ / ขอพักแป๊บ / ตามให้อยู่นะครับ

### Cross-unit / coordination-facing
Tone: polite and neutral.
Examples: รบกวนส่งข้อมูลครับ / ขอความคืบหน้าครับ / ขอเชิญครับ

### Junior/team-facing
Tone: friendly directive, not authoritarian.
Examples: ฝากดำเนินการครับ / ช่วยเช็กให้หน่อยครับ

## 7. Key Insight Hypotheses to Validate
- Utility stickers may outperform purely comedic stickers in send frequency.
- Work-safe humor may increase memorability and peer discovery.
- Short phrases (roughly 1–4 Thai words where possible) should improve legibility and speed.
- Administrative workflow language may differentiate the product more strongly than generic police visual motifs.
- The target may prefer a competent but relatable character over heroic or hyper-realistic police imagery.

## 8. Research Questions
Ask target users about behavior, not only preference:
1. What were the last 10 work messages you sent?
2. Which phrases do you type repeatedly every day?
3. What do you reply to a superior most often?
4. What phrases do you use to request or chase information?
5. What happens before, during, and after meetings?
6. Which work situations cause the most repeated chat messages?
7. Which phrases feel too stiff to type but would be comfortable as a sticker?
8. Which humor would be safe with peers but not with superiors?
9. Which of the prototype stickers would you use tomorrow, and to whom?
10. What phrase is missing?

## 9. Anti-Persona
Do not optimize Set 01 for:
- action/patrol policing
- arrest/chase/weapon scenarios
- rank/status display
- ceremonial imagery
- political or institutional advocacy

These may reduce relevance to the selected administrative-use case.
