PRODUCT / COMMUNICATION RULES
- Start from user need, real chat situation, communication utility and plausible buying/use motivation.
- Define target audience, JTBD, positioning and differentiation before production lock.
- Commercial potential comes from useful repeatable communication plus recognizable Character/personality, not decoration alone.
- Evaluate the full caption portfolio for frequent reusable chat needs, audience relevance, emotional range, distinctiveness, memorability/shareability and excessive redundancy.
- Do not keep weak captions merely to reach quantity. If captions are owner-locked, preserve them and separate improvement recommendations from source truth.
- Every Frame brief must define exact caption, sender intent, likely chat situation, tone/emotion, expression, gesture/action, minimum useful props, composition/camera guidance when material, and forbidden distractions.
- One Frame = one primary intent.
- Apply the internal 1-second test: meaning should be quickly understandable at small/chat-like viewing size.
- Captions must be natural, useful, tone-consistent and diverse across the set.
- Decorative detail must never outrank communication clarity.
- Hero Frames must test the riskiest/most representative dimensions of the set, including whether the Character/product feels distinctive and desirable enough to support a sellable product.
- Do not claim sales certainty. Gem A should maximize credible commercial potential using product evidence and owner intent, while actual market performance remains learned after release.

COMMERCIAL SCORECARD — REQUIRED
PRODUCT_POSITIONING.md must contain evidence-based commercial analysis, not a vague label such as "interesting" or "high potential" alone. Evaluate at minimum:
1. Audience clarity — who is most likely to buy/use and why.
2. Repeat-use utility — estimate how many captions are high-, medium-, and low-frequency in realistic chat use, with rationale.
3. Coverage — whether the set spans enough recurring situations without becoming generic.
4. Differentiation — why a buyer would choose this set over generic alternatives.
5. Signature territory — which captions/behaviors are strongly owned by this Character/concept.
6. Purchase hooks — the 3–7 strongest reasons/frames likely to attract attention, memory, sharing, or purchase.
7. Redundancy/cannibalization risk — captions that compete for the same job and how they differ.
8. Emotional range — whether the set covers practical, warm, humorous, supportive and relationship use appropriately for the target.
9. Character-market fit — whether the Character personality reinforces the intended audience and product promise.
10. Commercial risks — weak/generic captions, narrow use cases, visual monotony, unclear audience, or concept drift.
11. Recommended action — KEEP / IMPROVE / OWNER-DECISION, while preserving LOCKED source captions unless the owner explicitly unlocks them.

PORTFOLIO DISCIPLINE
- Distinguish utility anchors, relationship/emotional frames, signature concept frames, and memorable/humor frames.
- Avoid optimizing only for novelty; a sellable set needs both frequent-use utility and distinctive reasons to buy.
- Avoid optimizing only for generic utility; if every caption can belong to any sticker set, commercial differentiation is weak.
- Commercial scoring is diagnostic, not permission to silently rewrite owner-locked captions.
